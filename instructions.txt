FOLDING TRAVEL CUTLERY V9 — FULLY PRINTED PROTOTYPE
30 September 2026

START HERE
Print cutlery-v9-mechanism-test.3mf first to check the threads, clearances,
locking tabs and dismantling sequence before committing to the full set.
It contains three short test rings and the same barrel, cap, washer, keeper
and cover key used in the finished set. Those five hardware parts can be
reused if their printed fit is satisfactory. The cover itself is not included
in this small test, so cover retention still needs a separate physical check.

Use cutlery-v9-folding-prototype.3mf for the complete nine-part set, or the
individual files in print/. Both 3MFs are unsliced, printer-neutral models.
They contain no G-code or applied printer/material profile. Select your actual
printer and material. Dimensions are in millimetres. Do not mix v8 pivot parts
with v9 parts. The source/ directory contains the v7 input geometry with account metadata removed.

WHAT CHANGED
- A stationary barrel now guides the utensil rings throughout opening. Loosening
  the cap no longer withdraws their central guide.
- A recessed, removable keeper captures the cap and washer during normal use.
  Its left-hand thread allows deliberate removal for cleaning and servicing.
- Rigid cover arms attach to the barrel base with a removable quarter-turn key.
  End stops position the cover, which covers the unused heads during use.
- The key's flat finger head also fits the keeper's driver slot.
- Locking tabs have rounded roots and lead-ins, with matching pocket relief.
- Shallow assembly marks, direction arrows and a cover thumb pad were added.
- The repaired fork and 1.8 mm central spoon floor from v8 are retained.

This remains a shared-handle set: deploy one utensil at a time. The tabs couple
the utensils to one another at the folded and 180-degree deployed positions;
the tightened cap clamps the stack. The lowest ring rests on a smooth base.
The folded model, including cover and key, is 95.6 mm long, 58.4 mm wide and
34.0 mm high. The closed pivot itself is 31.6 mm high, including its base.

PARTS AND PRINT ORIENTATION
Print one each: fork, knife, spoon, washer, barrel, cap, keeper, cover, cover_key.
The 3MFs and individual STLs already have the intended print orientations:
- Utensils and washer: flat, pockets downward; utensil tabs upward.
- Barrel: broad base on the plate, guide barrel upward.
- Cap and keeper: inverted, with their outer top faces against the plate.
- Cover: closed end on the plate, long arms upward. Consider a brim.
- Cover key: long shaft horizontal, common flat face against the plate.

Objects are arranged within a 256 x 256 mm area with separated footprints.
Rearrange for another bed size. This layout does not account for the clearance
needed by a printer that prints complete objects one after another.

A 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.16 mm layers, four walls and 30% infill are starting values
carried from the supplied project's settings; they are not validated settings
for v9. Inspect the sliced threads, small pocket roofs, tabs, thin fork roots,
cover arm holes and bridges. Avoid support material trapped in moving joints.
The model has not been opened in a slicer or physically printed in this work.

ASSEMBLY
All clockwise/anticlockwise directions below are viewed from above the cap.

1. Place the barrel with its broad base down and cylindrical guide upward.
2. Add the fork, then knife, then spoon over the guide. The necks carry one,
   two and three shallow marks respectively. Tabs face upward, pockets down.
   Align all heads in the same direction. Seat each pair of tabs in the
   underside pockets of the next utensil.
3. Add the washer with its pockets downward and witness grooves upward.
   Seat it over the spoon tabs; check that every interface lies flat.
4. Fit the large cap and turn it CLOCKWISE by hand to clamp the seated stack.
   Do not use the cap to force a misplaced tab into a pocket.
5. Insert the small keeper through the cap's central opening. Use the flat
   finger head of the removed cover key in its slot. Turn the keeper
   ANTICLOCKWISE until its seating shoulder meets the barrel. Tighten gently.
   The keeper has a left-hand thread; the large cap has a right-hand thread.
6. Slide the cover over the aligned heads towards the pivot. Its arms fit over
   the base ears. Stop when the arm end stops meet the ears and holes align.
7. Insert the key through the aligned holes with its far-end lug lying flat
   in line with the horizontal keyways. Once it has passed through, turn it
   a quarter-turn so the lug is vertical, then pull the finger head gently
   outwards to seat the lug in the recess on the opposite arm.

The key is removable and can be lost. Its seated geometry blocks extraction,
but there is no spring to hold it seated. Physical handling and vibration
retention have not been established. Check that it remains seated before
relying on the cover as a grip or carrying the set loose in a bag.

OPENING AND CLOSING
1. Push the cover key's finger head inwards approximately 0.8 mm. Turn it a
   quarter-turn to align the far lug with the horizontal keyways, then pull
   it out. Slide the cover away from the pivot and off the utensil heads.
2. Loosen the large cap ANTICLOCKWISE approximately 1.68 turns. Its 2.5 mm
   pitch gives 4.2 mm of total release space. The keeper stays installed.
3. Spread the three keyed interfaces by hand: fork/knife, knife/spoon and
   spoon/washer. The geometry check uses 1.4 mm at each interface to clear
   the 1.2 mm tabs. The washer does not automatically rise with the cap.
4. Rotate the selected utensil 180 degrees on the fixed guide, keeping the
   others in their storage direction. Do not force engaged tabs to rotate.
5. Align and reseat all tabs and pockets. Tighten the cap CLOCKWISE gently.
   Check that the selected utensil stays indexed under gentle dry handling.
6. Refit the cover over the two unused heads and lock its key as above.
   The thumb pad lies on top of the cover; its arrow points towards the
   deployed utensil. Fold the utensil using the same release sequence.

The keeper stop is nominally 4.61 mm above the closed cap position; 4.6 mm was
checked clear in the model. Stop as soon as contact is felt. Do not force the
cap against the keeper or use the stop as a tightening reference.

DISMANTLING FOR CLEANING OR SERVICING
1. Remove the cover and its key. Align all heads and gently close the main cap.
2. Use the cover key's flat finger head to turn the central keeper CLOCKWISE.
   Withdraw it completely through the central opening. Keep it somewhere safe.
3. Turn the large cap ANTICLOCKWISE until it comes off. Lift off the washer,
   spoon, knife and fork. The barrel and base form one printed part.
4. Reassemble in the order above, including the keeper. Normal cap operation
   must not rely on an omitted or incompletely seated keeper.

FIRST PHYSICAL CHECKS
- Check that both threads start and turn by hand without binding.
- Confirm that the keeper seats fully and stops the cap before it can escape.
- Confirm that the washer remains guided and the rings can separate and rotate.
- Confirm that the tabs seat fully and resist unwanted rotation when clamped.
- Practise the complete removal and refitting of the keeper, cap and washer.
- On the full set, check the cover end stops, key release and retention, then
  repeat opening and closing to inspect wear, loosening and hand comfort.
Stop and adjust the fit if any part binds, cracks, rocks or needs excessive
force. Digital clearance does not establish a printed fit or working load.

PROTOTYPE STATUS
Printed fit, strength, wear and comfortable handling remain untested. Repeated
food contact, hot-food use, dishwasher cleaning and hygiene are unverified.
No food-contact suitability follows from a filament name or the source profile.

EDITABLE BUILD
The included scripts rebuild from source/pribor-cutlery-v7.3mf. They do not
require intermediate review files. Install the versions in requirements.txt
in a Python environment, then run from this folder:
  python build_cutlery.py
  python audit_v9.py
  python verify_package.py --rebuild
  python render_preview.py
The full set has nine parts; the separate mechanism test has eight. Do not
print both sets of identical hardware unless you want spares.
